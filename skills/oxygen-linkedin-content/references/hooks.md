# Hooks

The hook is line 1 plus the second beat: what a reader sees before "see more". Write it once the funnel job and the body's point are fixed. The author's current instructions and wording come first, then the canonical voice and their recent posts.

## Never your own product, offer or guest in line 1

Below the fold, name the product with specific capabilities. On 5 large accounts, an own offer or guest in line 1 opened 0–2 of the top 20 posts and 8–16 of the bottom 20. Exception: a curated list of other people to follow. If the user's analytics show otherwise, follow them and say so.

## Hook families

Families: result + "with this simple X"; test or comparison; big claim; news + result; confession + change; stakes; before and after; receipt as the promise. Fill numbers and names only from the `proof` page. Never use "Everyone is doing X. Almost nobody says Y."; readers recognise it as AI copy.

## Rotate the mechanism, not the template

Name the families of the last 2 published hooks (`oxygen publishing posts list --status published --order newest --limit 5 --json`) and use a different one. A repeated receipt moves to line 2. Re-arm a fading giveaway with a new artifact, never a bigger number.

## Length

House targets, not platform limits:

- Line 1: about 40 to 60 characters. A stronger 35- or 85-character line can be right.
- Essential opening: within 140 characters, spaces and newlines included, usually line 1 plus an informative second beat. Never hide the point after character 140.
- Inspect the first 210 characters as an extra stress check.

The 140 and 210 figures are working budgets, not LinkedIn's cutoff: wrapping, blank lines and the viewport move the fold. Never claim a character slice is the exact feed preview.

Whole post: LinkedIn's limit is 3,000 characters.

## Select

1. The second beat adds specificity, a consequence, a contrast or a clear promise; it never repeats line 1.
2. For options, vary the angle, not the adjectives. Offer 3 when options are asked for without a count; a full post needs 1 chosen hook.
3. Count the exact chosen text (line 1 and the opening block) and report the counts outside the copy. Move a late necessary word earlier; never pad a short hook.
